How prices are counted
  • We only use prices published on clinics' official websites. Each amount was re-checked on the official page when this page was built, and that date is shown as the check date.
  • 1 month means 4 weekly pens. Prices include tax as shown on each site; where a site says otherwise, it is noted in the details.
  • The total is the medicine price plus the consultation fee. Where the fee is not listed, we show "+ consultation fee not listed". English documents and shipping may cost extra.
  • For 2 and 3 months we use the clinic's own multi-month prices. Where the monthly price drops for longer prescriptions, we use that official price × the number of months.
  • If there is no 4-pen price we use the single-pen price ×4, and if there is no 2- or 3-month price we use the 1-month price × months. Those are marked "calculated".
  • First-purchase discounts and prices with an unclear quantity are left out.
  • Dollar amounts are approximate. The default rate is from Nov 16, 2026, and you can change it in the box above.

How a visitor gets Mounjaro prescribed in Japan

  1. Choose a clinic

    Find one in the city you are staying in that says it sees travelers, and check the price and hours on its official website.

  2. Book ahead

    Most clinics work by appointment. Send your preferred date and dose by web form, LINE or email. If you need an English prescription document, ask for it at the same time. It may cost extra.

  3. Bring your passport

    You do not need a prescription from home. If you already use it, show your current dose and records.

  4. See the doctor

    The doctor decides after the consultation whether to prescribe, which dose and how many pens.

  5. Pay and collect

    You usually pay in yen. Collect the pens at the clinic, or, where the clinic offers it, have them sent chilled to your hotel in Japan.

Collecting it on Tsushima

Tsushima is a short ferry ride from Busan, and trips there are usually short, so it pays to prepare early.

Decide where to receive it

If it goes to a hotel, ask first whether they can accept and keep a chilled parcel. You can also collect it at the courier's depot (営業所止め). Hitakatsu is in the north of the island and Izuhara in the south, far apart, so choose a place near the port you arrive at.

Order early

Deliveries to islands can take longer than to the mainland. Ask the clinic whether it ships chilled to a Tsushima address and how many days it takes, and leave plenty of time. If the clinic offers online consults, ask whether you can finish the consult before you travel.

Check the ferry

All ferries leave from Busan Port International Passenger Terminal. Izuhara sailings run only on certain days, so check the operator's official timetable first. Tsushima is part of Japan, so bring your passport.

Plan for cancellations

Bad weather sometimes cancels sailings across the Korea Strait. Agree with the clinic in advance whether the delivery date can move or the parcel can be re-sent to another address, and save the clinic's contact details.
